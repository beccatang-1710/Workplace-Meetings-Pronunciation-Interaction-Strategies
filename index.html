Task 1 • Pronunciation Strategies

Task 1: Word Stress & the Silent ‘e’ Rule

Understanding word stress and the silent ‘e’ pattern can help you speak clearly in workplace meetings. A stressed syllable stands out through greater prominence—often length, loudness, or a change in pitch. The silent ‘e’ pattern can change the vowel sound in a word.

Task 1a: Word Stress in Two-Syllable Nouns & Verbs

The same two-syllable word can sometimes be both a noun and a verb, and we stress them differently! *Note: Syllables are the units of pronunciation in a word. Usually, you can tell the number of syllables by the number of vowel sounds.

Pattern 1: NOUNS ● ○ (1st Syllable Stressed)

In two-syllable nouns, we usually put the stress on the first syllable.

Example: “We saw a sharp DEcrease in costs.”

Pattern 2: VERBS ○ ● (2nd Syllable Stressed)

In two-syllable verbs, we often put the stress on the second syllable.

Example: “We need to deCREASE plastic waste.”

Task 1c • Silent ‘e’ Rule

Pronunciation Strategy: The Silent ‘e’ Rule

A final ‘e’ is often silent. In the common vowel-consonant-e pattern, it signals that the preceding vowel usually has a “long” sound, as in mate and hope. A final e can also signal soft c or g, as in lace and stage. English spelling has exceptions.

Short ‘o’ vs Long ‘o’ (/ɒ/ → /əʊ/)
hop → hope
Short ‘a’ vs Long ‘a’ (/æ/ → /eɪ/)
plan → plane
Short ‘i’ vs Long ‘i’ (/ɪ/ → /aɪ/)
slid → slide

Task 2 • Multisyllabic Words

Task 2: Decoding Multisyllabic Words

In workplace meetings, many words contain more than one syllable, such as and . These are called multisyllabic words. Breaking them down by their syllables makes them much easier to pronounce!

📖 Syllable: A syllable is a unit of pronunciation, usually built around one vowel sound. A vowel team such as ai can represent a single vowel sound.

3 Steps to Break Down Multisyllabic Words:

1

Identify Prefixes & Suffixes

Spot familiar word beginnings (re-, eco-, under-) and endings (-able, -tion, -ment).

2

Identify Remaining Vowel Sounds

Count the vowel sounds in the root word (remembering vowel teams like ai or ea make one sound).

3

Pronounce Slowly, Then Blend

Say each syllable chunk slowly, then blend them smoothly with the correct word stress.

Task 2b • Vowel Sounds & Syllables

Task 2b: Identifying Vowel Sounds to Divide Syllables

Counting distinct vowel sounds (rather than vowel letters) is a useful first step for estimating syllables. Pronounce the word to check your count, using these tips:

Tip 1: Single Vowels

The word ‘cost’ contains one vowel sound → 1 syllable. In contrast, ‘carbon’ includes two vowel sounds → 2 syllables (car-bon).

Tip 2: Combined Vowels (1 Sound)

Sometimes two vowels combine to make one sound, such as ‘ai’ in sustain (2 syllables) and ‘ea’ in each (1 syllable). Count them as one vowel sound!

Tip 3: The Letter ‘Y’

At the start of these words, ‘y’ represents a consonant sound (yes, yellow). In words such as sky, recycle, and activity, it represents a vowel sound.

Task 3 • Sentence Stress

Task 3: Pronunciation Strategies — Sentence Stress

In English, sentence stress helps listeners follow the main idea. Content words carry much of the meaning and often receive stress; function words connect ideas and are often unstressed. Either type can be stressed for emphasis or contrast.

🔊 Content Words (often stressed)

Words that carry much of the sentence’s meaning, such as nouns, main verbs, adjectives, and adverbs. They often stand out through greater length, loudness, or pitch movement.

Task 5 • Interaction Strategies in Meetings

Task 5: Effective Interaction Strategies in Meetings

Scenario: You will attend a meeting with the volunteer coordinators to discuss ways to boost donations (and apply these strategies in your upcoming workplace meeting assessment). Learn these three essential interaction strategies to participate effectively:

Strategy A

a. Interrupting Politely

Politely interrupt a speaker when you have something important to add to the conversation.

• “Excuse me, could I add something here?”

• “May I jump in for a moment?”

Strategy B

b. Asking for Clarification

If you do not fully understand a point, do not hesitate to ask the speaker to clarify or give an example.

• “Could you please clarify what you mean by…?”

• “Can you give an example of…?”

Strategy C

c. Asking for Contributions

Make sure to engage all members in the discussion by asking for their thoughts or opinions.

• “I’d like to hear your thoughts on this, [Name].”

• “[Name], what do you think about this idea?”

Task 6: Check Understanding in Meetings

When a point is unclear or you want to confirm that others understand, ask a brief question or invite them to explain it in their own words. This helps prevent misunderstandings and keeps the meeting on track.
